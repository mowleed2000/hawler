Kurdish and Iraqi Food on Edgware Road: Finding Hawler

6 October 2026 · 8 min read

If you are hunting Kurdish and Iraqi charcoal cooking in central London, Edgware Road is the corridor — and Hawler is one of the addresses where the grill still leads.

Edgware Road has a reputation that precedes any single restaurant: shisha terraces, late grills, sweet shops, and the comforting density of Arabic, Kurdish, Iranian and Levantine kitchens within a short walk. That density is a gift to diners and a challenge to anyone choosing where to sit. This piece is a practical orientation toward Hawler Restaurant at 355 Edgware Road, London W2 1BS — a Kurdish and Iraqi charcoal grill house named for Hawler (Erbil).

What to expect from the neighbourhood

Come for atmosphere as much as coordinates. Groups spill across tables; families celebrate; solo diners take a late kebab after work. Public transport links are strong, and the strip stays lively into the evening. Hawler’s own hours — daily 12:00 to 23:00 — match that rhythm, covering long lunches and post-work grills alike. For maps and arrival tips, use the Visit page and call 020 7724 5757 if you need a reservation confirmed by phone.

Where Hawler sits on the flavour map

Among Middle Eastern options nearby, Hawler’s through-line is Kurdish-Iraqi charcoal and slow rice dishes. Signature markers include Kuzi Lamb Neck (£16), Slemani Kabab (£14.99), Mix 3 Kabab (£20.99) and lamb chops (£18), plus the dine-in welcome of complimentary soup, salad, hummus and Slemani naan. Everything is 100% Halal. If you want a primer before you travel, read the Kurdish food guide and the note on the Hawler name.

A feast that travels — or stays

Photographs of mixed grills with rice, bread, herb plates and dips capture how food looks when a table commits to sharing. You can recreate that abundance on site or order through Uber Eats, Deliveroo and Just Eat from Order. Dine-in remains the way to receive the complimentary welcome; delivery is the way to keep Edgware Road flavours on a W2 sofa when the weather or the timetable says stay in.

Who the address suits

  • Kurdish and Iraqi guests seeking familiar grill and Kuzi plates.
  • Londoners exploring beyond generic “mixed grill” menus.
  • Halal-observant diners wanting clarity without interrogation.
  • Small groups who want to share skewers and mezze.
  • Visitors staying near Paddington, Marylebone or Marble Arch who can reach the strip easily.

Building an Edgware Road evening

Arrive, accept the welcome dishes, order a mixed grill or Kuzi, keep bread moving, finish with tea. If the group is large, look at catering for what can be arranged — always confirm details with the restaurant rather than assuming banquet packages. Pair culture reading with Kurdish hospitality if you want the social “why” behind the bread and the shared platter.

Menu literacy in five minutes

Skim the menu on your phone on the train: grills, Kuzi and rice, cold mezze, kibbeh, weekend specials such as pacha or dolma when listed. Decide whether tonight is a fire night, a pot night, or both. That small bit of literacy means you spend your table time eating, not decoding.

The bottom line for Edgware Road

You have no shortage of places to eat on this street. Choose Hawler when you want Kurdish-Iraqi charcoal specifically, a Halal kitchen, and a welcome that starts before the order pad comes out. Address: 355 Edgware Road, London W2 1BS. Phone: 020 7724 5757. Hours: daily 12:00–23:00. The neighbourhood supplies the energy; the coals supply the reason to stop walking.

Getting there and timing your table

The W2 1BS stretch of Edgware Road is reachable from Paddington, Edgware Road station and several bus routes that stitch Marylebone to Hyde Park. Midweek lunches can be calmer; Friday and Saturday evenings carry more of the strip’s famous energy. Hawler’s open-daily pattern helps if your only free slot is a Sunday afternoon or a late Monday. Still, for groups, phone 020 7724 5757 rather than assuming a walk-in will always absorb you at peak.

Budget timing for complimentary starters, grill cooking and tea. A “quick kebab” mindset fights the hospitality model; a ninety-minute mindset fits it.

Comparing without turning dinner into a spreadsheet

Neighbourhood loyalty is personal. Some guests will always prefer another grill’s seasoning; others will return to Hawler for Kuzi, Slemani naan and the on-the-house welcome. Use this blog to understand categories — mixed grill, minced kebab, slow lamb, mezze — then judge with your own palate. The useful comparison is not a star rating scraped from the internet; it is whether the bread was hot, the charcoal tasted clean and you were treated like a guest.

When friends ask where to find Kurdish-Iraqi food near the Westway or Paddington, you can now answer with an address, a few dish names and a link to the menu — concrete help, not vague gestures toward “Edgware Road somewhere.”
