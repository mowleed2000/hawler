What Is Kuzi? The Slow Lamb Dish at the Heart of the Menu

28 July 2026 · 7 min read

Not every landmark dish at a Kurdish-Iraqi restaurant comes off the mangal. Kuzi — tender lamb over rice, finished with sauce — is the slow, generous plate guests order when they want depth rather than char.

Kuzi (you may also see Qozi or similar spellings) is a celebratory rice-and-lamb dish found across Iraqi and Kurdish tables. Recipes vary by household and city, but the idea holds: meat cooked until it softens, fragrant rice beneath or beside it, and a sauce that ties the plate together. At Hawler Restaurant on Edgware Road, Kuzi is the kitchen’s quiet signature — the dish that balances a menu otherwise famous for charcoal.

What lands on the plate

Hawler’s Kuzi Lamb Neck is priced at £16 and arrives with rice and one sauce. There is also a Kuzi Lamb Shank (£18.49) and a Kuzi Chicken (£16.49) for guests who want the same structure with a different protein. A Special Qozi (£18.99) offers baby lamb with rice and stew. In each case you are ordering a composed plate, not a lone skewer: grain, meat and sauce share the bowl.

The photograph that often represents Kuzi shows glossy, slow-cooked lamb over mixed rices — some plain, some tinted — with toasted almonds and raisins for contrast, and a small bowl of tomato-based stew on the side. That combination of savoury richness and gentle sweetness is part of why the dish feels festive even on an ordinary Tuesday.

How Kuzi differs from a kebab

Charcoal kebabs are about smoke, surface and speed. Fat drips, spices toast, and the meat is eaten soon after it leaves the fire. Kuzi asks for patience. The lamb is cooked until fibres loosen; the pleasure is in how easily it separates and how the sauce soaks into rice. Many tables order both: a grill to share and a Kuzi for whoever wants the braise. If you are still learning the wider menu, our guide to Kurdish flavours places Kuzi among starters, breads and mixed grills.

Choosing your sauce

Sauce is not an afterthought. Hawler lists several house options — among them okra, white beans, potato, aubergine (tabsee-style sauces appear too) — each changing the plate’s character. Okra brings a tangy, silky finish; white beans feel creamy and mild; potato sauce is comforting; spicier options wake the rice up. Ask your server what pairs best with lamb neck versus chicken if you are unsure. Extra sauces are also available if two people want different moods on the same order.

When to order Kuzi

Order Kuzi when you want a sit-down meal rather than a quick skewer run. It suits lunch as easily as dinner, and it is a thoughtful choice for guests who prefer soft textures to heavily charred crusts. Families often mix one Kuzi with a Slemani Kabab or a small mixed grill so every preference is covered. Because dine-in guests receive complimentary soup, salad, hummus and Slemani naan, you can keep the paid order focused — one generous Kuzi may be enough for a moderate appetite once the welcome dishes are counted.

Rice, nuts and the sense of occasion

Across the region, festive rice dishes are dressed with nuts and dried fruit — almonds, raisins, sometimes barberries elsewhere in the wider Middle East. Hawler’s plating nods to that tradition: the eye reads abundance before the first spoonful. It is the same visual language as a wedding or Eid spread, scaled to a restaurant plate. That is why Kuzi often becomes the “I’ve been thinking about this all week” order for regulars.

Practical details at Hawler

Hawler Restaurant serves Kuzi at 355 Edgware Road, London W2 1BS, daily from 12:00 to 23:00. The kitchen is 100% Halal. Reserve or check availability by calling 020 7724 5757. For a first visit focused on slow cooking, pair Kuzi with a cold mezze from the menu and leave charcoal exploration for the next meal — or reverse that order if smoke is what drew you in.

Prefer the sofa? The same dishes can be ordered through Uber Eats, Deliveroo and Just Eat via Order. Large gatherings can enquire through catering. Directions and transport tips live on the Visit page.

Kuzi in one sentence

If a friend asks what Kuzi is, you can say: slow-cooked lamb (or chicken) served with rice and a proper sauce — the comforting heart of an Iraqi and Kurdish table, cooked here under the name of Hawler, Erbil’s own name in Kurdish.

Then book the table, choose your sauce, and let the complimentary naan arrive while the kitchen finishes the pot.
