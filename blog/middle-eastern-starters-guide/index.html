Middle Eastern Starters Worth Ordering: Mezze, Salads and Kibbeh

8 September 2026 · 8 min read

Starters at a Kurdish-Iraqi table are not filler. They set the temperature of the meal — cool herbs against hot bread, lemon against smoke — and at Hawler some of them arrive before you order.

The most important starter at Hawler Restaurant may be the one you do not pay for. Dine-in guests receive complimentary soup, salad, hummus and hot Slemani naan — a welcome that changes how you build the rest of the order. Still, the cold and hot mezze list is worth knowing, because this is how tables extend the opening act when celebrating, lingering or feeding a crowd.

The complimentary welcome, explained

Soup, salad, hummus and Slemani naan appear as you settle. Treat them as the first course. Tear bread through hummus, eat salad for sharpness, let the soup take the edge off hunger. Then decide whether you need a second wave of mezze or can move straight to charcoal and Kuzi. Guests who ignore the welcome and over-order starters often leave food behind; guests who use it wisely order one or two extras for contrast.

Cold mezze and salads

From the menu (each typically served with a single baked naan when ordered as its own dish):

  • Fattoush (£4.49) — crisp fried bread bits with parsley and mint; bright and crunchy.
  • Tabbouleh (£5.50) — parsley-led salad with tomato, crushed wheat, onion, olive oil and lemon.
  • Hummus (£5.49) — chickpea, tahini, garlic and lemon (contains nuts as noted on the menu).
  • Baba ganoug (£5.49) — baked aubergine with sesame, tahini, yoghurt, garlic and lemon.
  • Jajick (£5.49) — cucumber in yoghurt; the cooler on a charcoal-heavy table.
  • Cheese salad (£4.49) and olives (£3.50) — simple, shareable extras.
  • Mix Platter (£18.99) — hummus, jajick, baba ganoug, tabbouleh and olives for the table.

A well-styled mezze spread — bread in the centre, bowls of fattoush, smoky aubergine, tabbouleh and hummus around it — is exactly the visual language of this course. Dip, scoop, pass, repeat.

Hot starters: kibbeh and falafel

When you want something fried and fragrant before the grill:

  • Potato, bulghur, rice kibbeh or Kibbeh Mosel (£6.00) — stuffed shells of spiced mince in different doughs.
  • Falafel (£5.00) — with salad and bread.
  • Fries (£4.00) — if the table needs a familiar crunch.

Kibbeh are especially good when one person at the table does not eat much grilled meat; they bring spice and texture without competing with a later Mix 3 Kabab.

How to pair starters with mains

  • With Slemani Kabab or any mixed grill: jajick and fattoush cut the smoke.
  • With Kuzi: tabbouleh or a sharp salad refreshes between spoonfuls of sauce and rice.
  • With a large group: one Mix Platter plus the complimentary welcome covers grazing while skewers cook.

Bread is part of the starter course

Naan is not a side in the British pub sense; it is a utensil. Complimentary Slemani naan begins the meal; extra loaves are listed under sides if you need them. Learn more in Kurdish and Iraqi bread.

A restrained starter strategy

For two diners: rely on the welcome, add one cold mezze if you want more herbs, then share a grill or two Kuzi plates. For four: add the Mix Platter or two kibbeh. For a first date with the cuisine: hummus and baba ganoug teach you the kitchen’s cold craft before the mangal arrives. Zoom out with the Kurdish food guide once starters make sense; the rest of the menu will feel shorter.

Acidity, fat and the job of a first course

Charcoal meat is rich. Good starters answer with acid (lemon, sumac-leaning salads, pickles where offered), bitterness and herbs (parsley-heavy tabbouleh), smoke of a different kind (aubergine in baba ganoug), or cool dairy (jajick). That is food science you can taste without naming it. If your main is Mix 3 Kabab, build the opening around brightness. If your main is Kuzi, a herb salad keeps the rice course from feeling heavy too soon.

Olive oil on hummus is not merely garnish; it carries aroma and softens the chickpea paste for bread. Pomegranate seeds on aubergine dips, when used, add sweet-tart pops against sesame depth. Notice those details and mezze stops being “small plates” and becomes the seasoning system for the whole meal.

Ordering mezze for the office or a catch-up

Starters shine when the gathering is social rather than strictly dinner-shaped. A Mix Platter, olives and tea can carry a long conversation before anyone commits to chops. Because Hawler is open from midday, a late lunch of mezze plus one shared grill is a civilised way to use Edgware Road without a formal evening reservation — though calling 020 7724 5757 remains wise when the group grows.
