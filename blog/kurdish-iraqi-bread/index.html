Slemani Naan and the Bread That Carries the Meal

15 September 2026 · 7 min read

Ask what you should never skip at a Kurdish-Iraqi grill and the honest answer is bread. Without it, dips stall and kebabs feel unfinished.

In many British restaurants bread is optional, charged, and forgotten at the edge of the table. In Kurdish and Iraqi dining it is structural. Hummus needs a scoop. Salad wants a wrap. Kebab juices ask for a soft, slightly charred surface to land on. At Hawler Restaurant the house answer is Slemani naan — and for dine-in guests, hot loaves arrive complimentary with soup, salad and hummus before mains are chosen.

What Slemani naan is like

Expect a round, blistered flatbread with puffed pockets and darker toasted spots from high heat. It should bend without cracking and tear with a little resistance. Fresh from the oven or griddle it smells faintly sweet and wheaty, ready to take olive oil, aubergine dip or a fragment of minced lamb. Photographs of bread beside open fire are not romantic exaggeration; heat is part of how the loaf gets its character.

On the Hawler menu

Alongside the complimentary welcome, the sides list includes Freshly Baked Naan (£1.50) and Freshly Baked Slemani Naan (£1.99). Many cold and hot starters are described as served with a single baked naan when ordered alone — so bread repeats through the meal by design. Grill dishes such as Slemani Kabab and Mix 3 Kabab also arrive with naan. You are not being upsold into carbs; you are being handed the regional utensil.

How to use it at the table

  • Tear, do not slice — irregular pieces hold dips better.
  • Line a piece with salad and kebab for a hand-held bite.
  • Use a soft centre to mop Kuzi sauce from the rim of the bowl.
  • Keep a hot loaf near the mezze and a second near the grill if the table is large.

If you care about the cold dishes bread is scooping, see the starters guide. If you care about what the bread is wrapping, start with Slemani Kabab.

Bread and charcoal: the same philosophy

Open fire appears twice in this cuisine: under skewers and in the life of the loaf. Both prize surface — blister, char, aroma — over industrial uniformity. Our charcoal grilling guide focuses on meat, but the aesthetic is shared: live heat, quick decisions, food that tastes of the method.

Why bread belongs in a “main” conversation

Tourists sometimes photograph only the skewer. Regulars watch for the loaf. A kebab that arrives without adequate bread feels incomplete in this tradition, the way certain Italian meals feel wrong without olive oil. Hawler builds bread into the welcome so you do not have to remember to ask. Remember anyway — and order another Slemani naan when the first basket disappears — because the grill is still going and the dips are not finished.

Bread carries the meal. Treat it that way, and the rest of the Kurdish-Iraqi table makes immediate sense.

Naan beside other breads you may know

British diners often arrive with pita, Iranian sangak or South Asian naan in mind. Slemani-style house bread sits in the wider flatbread family: soft, layered enough to pull apart, blistered where heat kissed the dough. It is typically less pillow-thick than some restaurant naans and more substantial than a cracker-thin wrap bread. The right loaf should survive a journey from hummus bowl to kebab plate without disintegrating, yet still feel tender.

Because so many Hawler starters include bread in the serve, you may accumulate pieces across courses. That is normal. Use older fragments for sturdy scooping and keep the hottest loaf for wrapping meat. Waste less by tearing smaller pieces than pride demands.

Bread, Halal cooking and family tables

For family meals, bread is the democratic food — elders, children and first-timers handle it with equal confidence. In a 100% Halal restaurant that already removes a layer of dietary anxiety for many guests, warm naan becomes the shared gesture everyone understands. Ask for an extra Slemani naan when the complimentary piece vanishes; at £1.99 it is one of the highest-leverage additions on the menu.

If you are ordering delivery, request bread explicitly and eat the grills as soon as they arrive so crust and loaf share the same moment. Reheated meat with fridge-cold bread is a different, lesser meal.
